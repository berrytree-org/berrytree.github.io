    Parents

    Explore in the Berry Family Timeline

    George Berry was born c. 1787 in Orange County North Carolina. George was killed in Texas before 1850. George had served in the North Carolina Militia in the war of 1812. He enlisted as a private in Capt. John Bradshaw’s Company in the North Carolina Militia. 1814 Muster: 4th Regiment, 7th Company. He was discharged as a private and his service made him eligible for land grants in Fayette County Alabama. George Berry’s service in Texas also made his heirs eligible for more land grants in Texas.

    Research Notes (July 2026)#

    Birth year. This page formerly gave “c. 1790.” Chapter 4 of Our Berrys in Frontier America gives 1787, stated twice, and that figure is followed here. Neither year is documented. The only primary constraint is the 1800 Orange County census, which placed George in the 10-to-15 age bracket (census day 4 August 1800), meaning he was born somewhere between about August 1784 and August 1790. That bracket accommodates both 1787 and 1790, and the census recorded brackets, not ages. The 1830 and 1840 Fayette County censuses do not discriminate either, since both candidate years fall in the same bracket in each. 1787 is preferred because it is the figure in the book, and because his sister Catherine was born in 1785, which makes George the first son two years behind her. It remains an inference, not a sourced date.

    Place of death. The family account holds that George was killed in Texas before 1850. The Fayette County probate minutes of 1852 and 1855, however, describe him as “late of said county deceased,” making Fayette County, Alabama his legal residence at death, and his estate was administered there. These are not necessarily in conflict, a man may die away from home, but the tension is unresolved. Letters of administration on his estate, not yet located, should settle it. The death place has therefore been left off the vital facts above pending that record.

    George Berry married Susanna Aldridge about 1814.

    Robert Berry Jr. was the bondsman for Peter Aldridge and Judith Waggoner February 4th 1786. They were the parents of Susanna Aldridge.

    Susanna Aldridge was born 1792 (1850 census). Susanna also inherited some property from her father in 1814. (see the estate record below)

    Susanna Aldridge Berry died between the 1850 census and 12 March 1855. She was enumerated as head of household in Fayette County, Alabama in 1850, aged 58, with her youngest son James. By 12 March 1855 the Probate Court describes her as “late of said County deceased,” with her son-in-law James H. Brasher serving as administrator and filing for a final settlement of the proceeds of her real estate. She held land in her own right, consistent with the property she inherited from her father Peter Aldridge. See the 1855 record below.

    Fayette County, Alabama Probate Records

    Estate of George Berry, Final Settlement, September Term 1851#

    George Berry’s Estate, in final settlement. Fayette County, Alabama Probate Court, September Term 1851 (click to enlarge)

    The Probate Court of Fayette County, Alabama decreed six equal distributive shares of $1,087.94, totalling $6,527.64. Henry Berry served as administrator, and was further ordered to pay the Judge of Probate ten dollars in balance of fees. Attested by Jno. C. Moore, Judge of Probate.

    HeirNamed in the record asPayment receipted
    John Berry“one of the heirs of said Estate”24 February 1855
    Henry Berry“one of the heirs”; as administrator, “authorized to retain in his own hands”retained
    George Berry“one of the minor heirs”7 April 1852, by James Ray, Guardian
    James Berry“one of the heirs”23 June 1852, by James H. Brasher, Guardian
    Moses Ray“the husband of Susan, one of the heirs”4 January 1853, signed with his mark
    James H. Brasher“the husband of Mary, one of the heirs”23 June 1852

    Every heir was paid between April 1852 and January 1853, except John Berry, who was living in Rusk County, Texas. His share did not reach him until 24 February 1855, nearly three years after his brothers and sisters.

    Two clauses in the decree are worth noting. George and James, both minors, were to be paid “or his legal representative.” And Moses Ray could not write his name; he signed the receipt with his mark, attested by the judge.

    Note on this citation. Benjamin Berry Henderson Sr. transcribed this record by hand at the Fayette County courthouse and cited it in his research as “Fayette County Ala Probate Records p 263,” without a repository reference. The record has now been located in a digitized image, and his transcription of the heirs is confirmed in every particular, six for six. His page number does not match this volume’s pagination and may refer to a final record book or to a published abstract; that remains unreconciled.

    Guardian’s Account for James Berry, 9 September 1852#

    Guardian’s account, George Berry’s Estate. Fayette County, Alabama Probate Court, 9 September 1852 (click to enlarge)

    “Come this day James H Brasher, Guardian for James Berry, one of the minor heirs of the Estate of George Berry late of said county deceased, and makes return of his account against said James Berry for the years 1850 & 1851…”

    The account was examined, approved, and ordered filed and recorded. Because Brasher was already accounting as guardian for 1850, the estate was in administration by that year at the latest, which supports a death before 1850.

    Final Settlements, 12 March 1855#

    George Berry’s Estate and Susan Berry’s Estate. Fayette County, Alabama Probate Court, 12 March 1855 (click to enlarge)

    Two entries, side by side, both filed on 12 March 1855.

    George Berry’s Estate. James H. Brasher filed his account current and vouchers for a final settlement of his guardianship over James Berry.

    Susan Berry’s Estate. James H. Brasher, now Administrator of the Estate of Susan Berry late of said County deceased, filed for a final settlement of the proceeds of her real estate. This is the record that establishes Susanna Aldridge Berry’s death.

    Both matters were set for 6 April 1855, both were ordered advertised in the Fayette Banner, and Elliott B. Jones was appointed guardian ad litem.

    The Guardianship Closed, 23 April 1855#

    Susan Berry’s Estate distributed, and the guardianship of James Berry closed. Fayette County, Alabama Probate Court, 23 April 1855, pp. 560-561 (click to enlarge)

    On 23 April 1855, James H. Brasher made final settlement of his guardianship over the youngest son, James Berry.

    Brasher chargeable with$1,669.15
    Less credits allowed$172.15
    Balance in his hands for the ward$1,497.00

    James’s inheritance from his father had been $1,087.94 in June 1852. By April 1855 the fund had grown to $1,669.15, an increase of $581.21 in under three years. That is more than legal interest alone would produce, so Brasher was receiving something else on the boy’s behalf. Rents, hires, we do not yet know.

    And the guardian ad litem earned his appointment. Elliott B. Jones, put there by the court to protect a nineteen-year-old’s interest against his own brother-in-law, read the account and challenged one item:

    “the said Elliott B Jones, Guardian as aforesaid, objected to the allowance of voucher No. 1 for thirty dollars, which objection was sustained by the court, and the allowance of said voucher rejected”

    Had that voucher passed, James would have received $1,467.00. Because it was rejected, he received $1,497.00. Jones’s objection was worth exactly thirty dollars to him.

    James turned 21 on 3 August 1856, so the guardianship closed fifteen months early. He married Mary Elizabeth McClure in Rusk County, Texas that same year, in the county where his eldest brother John was living. A boy settling his accounts and leaving for Texas is a plausible reading of it, but it is not proven.
